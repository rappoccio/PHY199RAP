The Levelized Cost of Energy

Comparing how much a kilowatt-hour really costs

PHY199-RAP · Energy in the 21st Century

What is LCOE?

The levelized cost of energy is the average price per unit of electricity that pays back all lifetime costs of a plant.

\[ \text{LCOE} = \frac{\text{lifetime costs}}{\text{lifetime energy produced}} \]

It lets us compare a solar farm, a gas plant, and a reactor on the same footing — dollars per megawatt-hour.

A simple calculation

A solar farm costs $1,000,000 to build, runs 25 years, and makes 2,500 MWh/year.

\[ \text{LCOE} = \frac{\$1{,}000{,}000}{25 \times 2{,}500\ \text{MWh}} = \$16\,/\text{MWh} \]

Real estimates add fuel, maintenance, and a discount rate — but the idea is the same.

Rough LCOE by source

SourceTypical LCOE ($/MWh)

Cheapest on paper isn't the whole story — land, waste, and human cost don't appear here.

Takeaway

LCOE is a powerful first comparison —

but a fair energy choice weighs the costs it leaves out.
